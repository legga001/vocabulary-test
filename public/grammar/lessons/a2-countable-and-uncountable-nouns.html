A2 grammar

Countable and uncountable nouns

Use a number with countable things. For uncountable things, use an expression such as a piece of or a bottle of when you need a unit.

The pattern

a/an or a number + countable noun; some or a unit + uncountable noun

Work through an example

  1. You want to order rice.
  2. Rice usually describes an amount, so say “some rice”.
  3. For an exact size, name a countable unit: “two bowls of rice”.

More examples

Two apples, some bread and a bottle of water, please.

  • Three peaches and some yoghurt.
  • A piece of advice helped me.
  • How much luggage have you got?
  • We ordered two slices of pizza.

What to watch for

  • Some words have both uses: “a chicken” is a bird, while “some chicken” usually means meat.
  • Information, furniture and advice are normally uncountable in English.
  • “Two coffees” usually means two servings.

Your turn

Fix “Can you give me an advice?”

Reveal the answer

Can you give me some advice? You can also say “a piece of advice”.
