B2 grammar

Non-defining relative clauses

Use commas around extra information. Use who, which or whose, and keep the relative word in the clause. Use which to refer back to a whole idea.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Imagine putting extra information between two small pauses. If the listener already knows which person or thing you mean, commas show that the relative clause is an added detail.

The pattern

noun + comma + who/which/whose clause + comma if the sentence continues

How does it work?

1. Put extra information between commas

  1. You know which dog I mean: my dog Bruno.
  2. Add the extra detail that he hates rain.
  3. Write “Bruno, who hates rain, refuses to go outside.”

2. Use whose for a relationship or possession

Add whose bike is red after Mia → “Mia, whose bike is red, led the way.”

3. Use which to refer back to a whole idea

In “We missed the bus, which was annoying”, which stands for the event of missing it.

More examples

Mia, whose bike is red, led the way. We missed the bus, which was annoying.

  • Our roof, which was repaired last year, is leaking.
  • Eli, whose sister lives nearby, offered us a lift.
  • The bus never came, which surprised us.

What to watch for

  • A non-defining clause uses who, which or whose here. Keep the relative word and avoid that.
  • “My cousins who live nearby” picks one group. “My cousins, who live nearby,” adds a detail about all the cousins meant.
