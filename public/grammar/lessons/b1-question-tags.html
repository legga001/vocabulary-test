B1 grammar

Question tags

Use a short question at the end to check or invite agreement. A positive statement usually takes a negative tag, and a negative statement takes a positive one.

The pattern

statement + auxiliary or do + matching pronoun; usually reverse positive and negative

Work through an example

  1. Start with “The cat is asleep.”
  2. Repeat is and make it negative: isn’t.
  3. Replace the cat with it: “The cat is asleep, isn’t it?”

More examples

You’ve met Sam, haven’t you? It isn’t far, is it?

  • You can swim, can’t you?
  • They haven’t arrived, have they?
  • Ada left early, didn’t she?
  • The lights aren’t on, are they?

What to watch for

  • Use do, does or did if there is no helping verb in a simple-tense statement.
  • “I’m next, aren’t I?” is a common exception.
  • A rising voice often asks a real question. A falling voice often seeks agreement.

Your turn

Add a tag: “You two like puzzles, ___?”

Reveal the answer

You two like puzzles, don’t you? Use do because like is in the present simple.
