A1 grammar

Place and time prepositions

Use in for an enclosed space or a month, on for a surface or a day, and at for a point or a clock time.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Think of at as a point, on as a surface or day, and in as a space or a longer period. A few everyday phrases are fixed, so it helps to learn them together.

The pattern

Place: in a space, on a surface, at a point. Time: in a period, on a day or date, at a clock time.

How does it work?

  1. You’re planning to meet at 8 on Monday in June.
  2. 8 is a time on the clock, so choose at.
  3. Monday is a day and June is a month: “at eight on Monday in June”.

More examples

My keys are on the table. See you at six on Friday!

  • She’s in the lift.
  • The note is on the fridge.
  • Meet me at the gate.
  • We start on 3 May.
  • It snows in winter.

What to watch for

  • Remember at night, in the morning and on Friday morning.
  • Say “See you next Friday” without on.
  • British English often uses at the weekend. American English often uses on the weekend.
