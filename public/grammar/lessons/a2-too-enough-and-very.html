A2 grammar

Too, enough and very

Very makes an adjective stronger. Too means more than you want or need. Put enough after an adjective or before a noun.

The pattern

very + adjective; too + adjective; adjective + enough; enough + noun

Work through an example

  1. A bag is difficult to carry: “It’s very heavy.”
  2. You cannot lift it at all: “It’s too heavy to lift.”
  3. Your friend can lift it: “She’s strong enough.”

More examples

It’s very cold. It’s too cold to swim. The water isn’t warm enough.

  • The soup is very salty.
  • The soup is too salty for me.
  • We haven’t got enough plates.
  • The garden is big enough for a party.

What to watch for

  • Too often points to a problem. Very simply makes a description stronger.
  • Before a noun, use too many for countable things and too much for amounts.
  • At the end of a sentence, too can also mean “also”.

Your turn

Fix “There aren’t chairs enough.”

Reveal the answer

There aren’t enough chairs. Enough goes before a noun.
