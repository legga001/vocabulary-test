B1 grammar

May, might and could

Use these modals plus the base verb for possibilities. A time expression can make it clear whether you mean now or later.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says You can use a modal to leave room for another possibility. If you want to say that someone possibly won’t do something, might not often makes the meaning easy to hear.

The pattern

may/might/could + base verb; may/might not + base verb

How does it work?

  1. You can’t find your keys and think of one possible place.
  2. Choose might to show you’re unsure.
  3. Say “The keys might be in my coat.”

More examples

Jo might be at home now. The match could start late tonight.

  • We may stop for lunch.
  • The library could be closed.
  • It might not rain after all.
  • She may be working right now.

What to watch for

  • Could can also express past ability: “At ten I could swim.” Use context to tell the meanings apart.
  • May can give permission in “You may leave.”
  • To say something is possibly untrue, use might not. Couldn’t often means you think it’s impossible.
