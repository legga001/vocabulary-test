B2 grammar

Past advice and missed opportunities

Use should have to talk about a better past action. Could have can describe an opportunity someone had but didn’t take. Needn’t have means the action happened and was unnecessary.

The pattern

should/could/needn’t + have + past participle

Work through an example

  1. You bought tickets, but your friend already had some for you.
  2. Your purchase happened and was unnecessary.
  3. Say “I needn’t have bought tickets.”

More examples

I should have packed a coat. I could have borrowed one. You needn’t have bought me a new one!

  • I should have checked the address.
  • We could have taken a shortcut, but we missed it.
  • He shouldn’t have shared that photo.
  • You needn’t have waited up.

What to watch for

  • “I didn’t need to wait” leaves open whether I waited. “I needn’t have waited” says I did.
  • Could have can also mean something possibly happened.

Your turn

You left without a map and got lost. Give yourself past advice.

Reveal the answer

I should have brought a map. It describes a better choice in the past.
