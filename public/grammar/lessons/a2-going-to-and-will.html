A2 grammar

Going to and will

Use be going to for an existing plan or a prediction based on evidence. Will often marks a decision made as you speak, or a promise.

The pattern

am/is/are going to + base verb; will + base verb

Work through an example

  1. You’ve already bought flour to make bread.
  2. Say “I’m going to make bread.” The plan exists already.
  3. A friend says there’s no butter. You decide now: “I’ll get some.”

More examples

I’m going to bake a cake tonight. You forgot the eggs? I’ll get some.

  • Look at that dark cloud. It’s going to rain.
  • I’ll lend you my umbrella.
  • I think the blue team will win.
  • We aren’t going to stay long.

What to watch for

  • Both forms can make predictions. Going to often draws attention to evidence you can see now.
  • Going to here needs a verb after to. “I’m going to Leeds” uses go as a movement verb.

Your turn

You see a glass about to fall. Choose “It’s going to fall” or “I’ll catch it” for what you decide to do.

Reveal the answer

I’ll catch it. You’re making the decision now.
