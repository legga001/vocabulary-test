C1 grammar

Hedging claims

Use modals and phrases such as appears to or is likely to to signal how much certainty your evidence supports. Different choices imply different levels of confidence.

The pattern

may/might + base verb; appears to + base verb; is likely to + base verb

Work through an example

  1. Start with a strong claim: “The new timetable caused the delays.”
  2. You know the dates match, but you don’t know the cause.
  3. Write “The new timetable may have contributed to the delays.”

More examples

The delay may have affected the result. The delay is likely to have affected the result.

  • The number appears to be falling.
  • These results suggest a link.
  • Demand is likely to rise.
  • We cannot rule out a recording error.

What to watch for

  • May signals a possibility; is likely to signals a stronger expectation.
  • Choose the hedge that matches the evidence, and avoid stacking several vague hedges together.
  • A confident statement can still give its source or limits.

Your turn

Soften “The change improved attendance” when the evidence is incomplete.

Reveal the answer

“The change may have improved attendance.” May have marks a possible past effect.
