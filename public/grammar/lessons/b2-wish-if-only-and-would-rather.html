B2 grammar

Wish, if only and would rather

Use wish plus a past form for a present situation you want to change, or wish plus the past perfect for a regret. After I’d rather plus a different subject, use a past form for a preference about now or later.

The pattern

wish/if only + past for now; past perfect for earlier; would rather + person + past form for a present preference

Work through an example

  1. You have a noisy room and want quiet now.
  2. Say “I wish it were quieter.”
  3. You regret booking it yesterday: “If only I’d read the reviews first!”

More examples

I wish I had more time. If only I’d started earlier! I’d rather you waited outside.

  • I wish I knew the answer.
  • I wish the music would stop.
  • I’d rather you called tomorrow.
  • I’d rather walk home.

What to watch for

  • With the same subject, use would rather plus the base verb: “I’d rather wait.”
  • Use hope for a future outcome that’s still open: “I hope the bus comes soon.”
  • Were is common in formal wish constructions, and was is common in conversation.

Your turn

You regret leaving your umbrella at home. Start “I wish…”

Reveal the answer

I wish I’d brought my umbrella. Use the past perfect for a regret about an earlier choice.
