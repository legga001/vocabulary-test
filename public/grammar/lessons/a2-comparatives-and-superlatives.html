A2 grammar

Comparatives and superlatives

Use -er or more to compare things and -est or most to pick the one at the top of a group. Good changes to better and best.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says When you say than, you are comparing one thing with another. When you pick one thing from a whole group, show that with a superlative such as the longest.

The pattern

comparative + than; the + superlative + group or context

How does it work?

1. Compare things and choose the top one

  1. Compare your bag with mine: “My bag is heavier than yours.”
  2. Heavy changes y to i before er.
  3. Compare all the bags: “Mine is the heaviest bag here.”

2. Use more and most with longer adjectives

Choose more expensive for two phones → “This phone is more expensive.” Choose the most expensive for the whole shop.

3. Learn good and bad separately

Change good to better to compare → “This map is better than mine.” Use best for the top choice.

More examples

This route is shorter than the other one. It’s the quickest way home.

  • This hill is steeper than that one.
  • The red jacket is more expensive.
  • Sam had the best idea.
  • This is the least crowded train.

What to watch for

  • Good → better → best and bad → worse → worst.
  • Big → bigger → biggest. Nice → nicer → nicest.
  • Use “more useful”, rather than adding both more and er.
