A2 grammar

Present continuous for arrangements

Use am, is or are plus -ing when a future plan is already arranged. A future time expression makes the meaning clear.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Try asking “Have we arranged this with someone, or booked it?” If yes, the present continuous can do double duty and talk about the future. Add when so your listener knows which meaning you intend.

The pattern

am/is/are + -ing + future time or context

How does it work?

1. Talk about an arrangement

  1. You and Sana agreed to meet on Saturday.
  2. Choose “We’re meeting” because the plan is arranged.
  3. Name the time and place: “We’re meeting at the library on Saturday.”

2. Ask about someone’s arrangement

Put are before you, then add working tomorrow → “Are you working tomorrow?”

3. Tell someone about a timetable

For a published timetable, choose leaves → “The train leaves at six tomorrow.”

More examples

I’m meeting Jo at the station tomorrow.

  • I’m flying out on Friday.
  • Are you working tomorrow?
  • We aren’t staying overnight.
  • The dentist is seeing me at four.

What to watch for

  • Without a future phrase or clear context, “I’m eating” usually means now.
  • For a timetable, the present simple is common: “The train leaves at six.”
  • A loose intention can use going to.
