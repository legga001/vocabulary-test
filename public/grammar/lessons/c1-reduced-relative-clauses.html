C1 grammar

Reduced relative clauses

Use an -ing form for many active descriptions and a past participle for many passive ones. Attach the phrase directly to the noun it describes.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Ask whether the noun does the action or has the action done to it. A person waiting is doing something. A letter written yesterday received the action. Keep the longer clause if the timing would get lost.

The pattern

noun + -ing form for an active description; noun + past participle for many passive descriptions

How does it work?

1. Shorten an active relative clause

  1. Take “The visitors who are waiting outside have tickets.”
  2. The visitors are doing the waiting, so use waiting.
  3. Write “The visitors waiting outside have tickets.”

2. Shorten a passive relative clause

Start with “The forms that were signed are here.” Keep signed → “The forms signed yesterday are here.”

3. Keep the full clause when a modal matters

In “the devices that must be replaced”, keep the full form so the reader sees the requirement.

More examples

The fans waiting outside have tickets. The tickets printed yesterday have the wrong date.

  • The bags left under the seats are ours.
  • Anyone needing a lift should call me.
  • The cake made this morning has already gone.
  • Rae, wearing a bright coat, was easy to spot.

What to watch for

  • A shortened clause can lose tense detail. Keep “the people who arrived yesterday” if “people arriving yesterday” sounds unclear.
  • Keep a full clause when the modal matters: “the devices that must be replaced”.
