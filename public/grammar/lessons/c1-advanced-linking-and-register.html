C1 grammar

Advanced linking and register

Use whereas to set two clauses against each other. Use nevertheless to signal an unexpected continuation in formal writing. In a casual message, still often sounds more natural.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Think first about the connection you want your reader to make. Are you comparing two things, saying something surprising happened anyway, or replacing one choice with another? Then choose a connector that suits the reader and the situation.

The pattern

whereas + clause for comparison; nevertheless or still starts a sentence that gives an unexpected result

How does it work?

1. Choose a formal or everyday link

  1. Take two facts: “The task was difficult. We finished early.”
  2. For a formal report, use “The task was difficult. Nevertheless, we finished early.”
  3. For a message to a friend, “It was hard. Still, we finished early” is natural.

2. Compare two facts within one sentence

Use whereas to connect the facts → “The north path is steep, whereas the south path is gentle.”

3. Use instead for a change of plan

When one choice replaces another, put instead with the new one → “The café was shut. We went to the park instead.”

More examples

The trail was steep. Nevertheless, we reached the top before noon. → It was steep. Still, we made it before noon.

  • The north path is steep, whereas the south path is gentle.
  • Tickets were expensive. Even so, the hall was full.
  • The first plan saved time. By contrast, the second saved money.
  • They were tired, yet they kept walking.

What to watch for

  • Whereas compares two ideas within a sentence. Nevertheless can connect two complete sentences with different punctuation.
  • Use instead when something replaces an option, rather than for any kind of contrast.
