B1 grammar

Zero and first conditionals

For general results, use the present simple in both clauses. For a real future possibility, use if plus the present simple, then will plus the base verb for the result.

The pattern

general rule: if + present, present; future possibility: if + present, will/can/may/command

Work through an example

  1. Think of a rule: cold enough water freezes. Say “If water gets cold enough, it freezes.”
  2. Now think of tomorrow’s picnic. “If it rains tomorrow, we’ll eat indoors.”
  3. The if part sets the condition. The other part tells you the result.

More examples

If ice gets warm, it melts. If it rains tomorrow, we’ll play indoors.

  • If you heat the pan, the butter melts.
  • If I finish early, I can help you.
  • If the alarm sounds, leave the building.
  • We’ll take the bus if it rains.

What to watch for

  • Use a comma when the if clause comes first.
  • Unless means if not in many cases: “We’ll go unless it rains.”
  • Often use a present form after if for future conditions.

Your turn

Choose for tomorrow’s trip: “If I miss the train, I take / I’ll take the bus.”

Reveal the answer

I’ll take the bus. This is a future backup plan.
