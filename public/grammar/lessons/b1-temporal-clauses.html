B1 grammar

Temporal clauses

After when, as soon as or until, use the present simple in clauses about many future situations. The main clause can use will.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says A sentence about the future can still use a present form in the time clause. If one half answers “When?”, look there first for when, until or as soon as.

The pattern

future main clause + when/as soon as/until + present form

How does it work?

1. Use when to give future timing

  1. You plan to unlock the door, then send a message.
  2. Use will for the promise: “I’ll text you”.
  3. Use present simple for the timing: “I’ll text you when I get inside.”

2. Use until to show when something ends

Keep stops in the present form → “Wait until the rain stops.”

3. Keep will inside an indirect question

In “I wonder when the rain will stop”, you are asking about the time. Will works here.

More examples

I’ll text you as soon as I arrive. Wait here until I get back.

  • We’ll eat as soon as the bread is ready.
  • Don’t leave until the rain stops.
  • Before you go, check the map.
  • After you’ve signed, I’ll send a copy.

What to watch for

  • An indirect question can use will: “I don’t know when the bus will arrive.” Here when asks what time.
  • Until tells you when something ends. By gives a deadline.
