C1 grammar

Cleft sentences

Use it was … that or what … is to put a particular detail under the spotlight. The rest of the sentence supplies the context.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Imagine someone got part of a story wrong. You can use a cleft to replace just the detail they missed: who, when, where, or what mattered. The rest of the sentence stays familiar.

The pattern

it + be + focus + who/that clause; what-clause + be + focus

How does it work?

1. Use it was to focus on a person

  1. Start with “Leah found the missing page.”
  2. Choose what matters here: who found it.
  3. Put the name after it was: “It was Leah who found the missing page.”

2. Use it was to focus on a time or place

Start with “We met at the station.” Move at the station after it was → “It was at the station that we met.”

3. Use what to focus on the thing needed

Start with “I need rest.” Use what I need is → “What I need is rest.”

More examples

It was Jo who found the key. What I need is a good night’s sleep.

  • It was on Tuesday that I saw it.
  • What they need is a quieter room.
  • What surprised me was how calmly she spoke.
  • It wasn’t the price that bothered me.

What to watch for

  • Use who or that for a person in an it-cleft. That normally works for a time or place.
  • In “What I need is rest”, what means the thing that. It introduces the focus structure instead of a question.
