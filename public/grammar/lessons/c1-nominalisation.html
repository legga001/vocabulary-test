C1 grammar

Nominalisation

Turn an action into a noun phrase to make it the topic of a sentence. Name the person doing the action when that information matters to your reader.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Turn an action into a noun when you want to talk about the action itself. Keep the person or group who did it if the reader needs to know who was responsible. A short plain verb may still be the clearest choice in a busy sentence.

The pattern

verb or adjective → noun phrase; keep the actor if it helps

How does it work?

  1. Start with “The lab tested the water. The tests took two weeks.”
  2. Turn tested the water into a noun phrase: “the lab’s testing of the water”.
  3. Use the noun phrase as the topic: “The lab’s testing of the water took two weeks.”

More examples

The council approved the plan. The council’s approval allowed work to begin.

  • The team decided to postpone → the team’s decision to postpone.
  • The bridge failed → the failure of the bridge.
  • The staff were able to respond quickly → the staff’s quick response.

What to watch for

  • A noun can hide who acted: “Mistakes were made” and “the failure” give little information by themselves.
  • Too many nouns in a row can slow a sentence down. Keep a verb when action matters most.
