B2 grammar

Past deduction

Use must have for a strong inference, might have for a possibility and can’t have for a past event you think was impossible. Follow each with a past participle.

The pattern

must/might/may/could/can’t + have + past participle

Work through an example

  1. You see wet footprints in the hall.
  2. You strongly guess someone came in from the rain.
  3. Say “Someone must have come in from the rain.”

More examples

The gate is open. Someone must have unlocked it. It can’t have been Jo. She’s away.

  • She might have missed the bus.
  • They can’t have walked here in two minutes.
  • I may have left the oven on.
  • The timer could have stopped overnight.

What to watch for

  • Must have can express a strong guess, while had to expresses a past duty.
  • Could have can also describe an unused opportunity. The sentence around it helps you decide.

Your turn

A concert is still happening. Complete “It ___ have ended an hour ago.”

Reveal the answer

It can’t have ended an hour ago. You have evidence that rules out that idea.
