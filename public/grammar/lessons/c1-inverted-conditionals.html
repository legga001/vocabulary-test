C1 grammar

Inverted conditionals

In formal writing, omit if and place had, were or should before the subject in certain conditionals. The time and meaning of the condition stay the same.

The pattern

had + subject + participle; were + subject + to or complement; should + subject + base verb

Work through an example

  1. Take “If I had read the note, I would have called.”
  2. Remove if and move had before I.
  3. Write “Had I read the note, I would have called.”

More examples

Had I known, I’d have called. Were the plan to change, we’d let you know.

  • Should you need a copy, email us.
  • Were the road to close, we could take the train.
  • Had they checked the address, they’d have found us.
  • Were it not for the delay, we’d be home now.

What to watch for

  • This pattern sounds formal. Ordinary if clauses are more common in conversation.
  • Use “Had I not known”, with not after the subject in this pattern.
  • Keep if with ordinary verbs: “If it rains”, unless you choose the should pattern.

Your turn

Rewrite “If you should change your mind, call me” without if.

Reveal the answer

Should you change your mind, call me. Move should before you.
