C1 grammar

Participle clauses

An -ing clause can add an action or reason. Having plus a past participle signals an earlier action. The understood subject should match the subject of the main clause.

The pattern

-ing for an active detail; having + participle for an earlier action; past participle for many passive details

Work through an example

  1. Start with “After we locked the gate, we went home.”
  2. The same people did both actions. Use “Having locked the gate” for the earlier one.
  3. Write “Having locked the gate, we went home.”

More examples

Having checked the map, we took the next turn. Waiting outside, I heard the bell.

  • Seeing the queue, Mina decided to wait outside.
  • Having missed the last train, we walked home.
  • Built on a steep slope, the house needs strong supports.

What to watch for

  • “Walking home, the rain started” makes it sound as if the rain was walking. Say “As I was walking home, it started to rain.”
  • A full clause often gives a clearer time or reason when the link is ambiguous.

Your turn

Shorten “After I checked the map, I turned left.”

Reveal the answer

Having checked the map, I turned left. The same I did both actions.
