B1 grammar

Present perfect continuous

Use have or has been plus -ing to emphasise an activity or its duration. The simple form often emphasises a completed result.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Imagine a friend asks what you’ve been up to. You can talk about the activity, or you can count what you finished. Choose the form that answers their question.

The pattern

have/has been + -ing; have/has + past participle for a result

How does it work?

1. Choose the activity or the result

  1. Imagine flour on your sleeves. You explain what you’ve been doing: “I’ve been baking.”
  2. To tell someone how much you finished, give the result: “I’ve baked three loaves.”
  3. Both connect the past to now, but they answer different questions.

2. Explain a clue you can see now

Your hands are muddy. Use have been digging → “I’ve been digging in the garden.”

3. Use the simple form with a continuing state

Choose have known for a state that continues → “I’ve known Li for years.”

More examples

I’ve been reading all afternoon. I’ve read three chapters.

  • I’ve been trying to call you all morning.
  • It’s been raining. The ground is wet.
  • We’ve painted two rooms.
  • She hasn’t been sleeping well.

What to watch for

  • State verbs generally take the simple form: “I’ve known Ada for years.”
  • The continuous can describe something that just stopped, so it may no longer be happening.
