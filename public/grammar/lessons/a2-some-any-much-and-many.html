A2 grammar

Some, any, much and many

Use some in many positive statements and offers, and any in many questions and negatives. Many goes with countable plurals. Much goes with uncountable nouns.

The pattern

some/any + plural countable or uncountable nouns; many + plurals; much + amounts

Work through an example

  1. You’re checking what’s left before baking.
  2. Ask “How many eggs are left?” Eggs can be counted.
  3. Ask “How much sugar is left?” Sugar is an amount.

More examples

We have some eggs. Are there any tomatoes? How much milk is left? How many eggs?

  • We bought some lemons.
  • We haven’t got any flour.
  • Would you like some tea?
  • I don’t have much time.
  • There are a lot of seats.

What to watch for

  • In offers and requests, some is common: “Can I have some water?”
  • Any can mean it doesn’t matter which: “Take any seat.”
  • In everyday positive statements, a lot of often sounds more natural than much.

Your turn

Complete “How ___ chairs do we need?”

Reveal the answer

How many chairs do we need? Chairs is a plural countable noun.
