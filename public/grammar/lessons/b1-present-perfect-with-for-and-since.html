Present perfect with for and since

Use the present perfect for a situation that started in the past and continues now. For gives the length of time. Since gives the starting point.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Imagine a line from the past to now. Put since at the point where it starts. Use for to measure its length. If the situation has finished, the same word for can still work with the past simple.

The pattern

have/has + past participle + for a length or since a start

How does it work?

  1. Nell moved here in 2020 and still lives here.
  2. Use the present perfect because the situation continues: “Nell has lived here”.
  3. Add the starting point: “Nell has lived here since 2020.”

More examples

I’ve lived here for three years. I’ve lived here since 2023.

  • We’ve known each other for six years.
  • I haven’t seen her since Tuesday.
  • It’s been cold since we arrived.
  • How long have you worked here?

What to watch for

  • Use the past simple if the situation has ended: “I lived there for six years. Then I moved.”
  • Since can introduce a starting event as well as a date.
