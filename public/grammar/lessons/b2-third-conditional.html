B2 grammar

Third conditional

Use if plus had and a past participle for an imagined change to the past. Use would have plus a past participle for its imagined result.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Start with what actually happened. Then change one piece of the past in your imagination and say what that would have changed. You’re looking back at a door that has already closed.

The pattern

if + had + past participle; would/could/might have + past participle

How does it work?

  1. We missed the ferry because we left late.
  2. Imagine a different past: “If we’d left on time”.
  3. Imagine the result: “If we’d left on time, we would have caught the ferry.”

More examples

If we’d left earlier, we would’ve caught the train.

  • If I’d seen the sign, I’d have stopped.
  • We might have won if we’d trained more.
  • If the lift had worked, we could have used it.

What to watch for

  • In “If I’d known, I’d have helped”, the first ’d means had and the second means would.
  • Use might have if the result is only possible.
  • Would have normally belongs in the result clause.
