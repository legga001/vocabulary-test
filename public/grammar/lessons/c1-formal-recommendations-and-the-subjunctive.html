Formal recommendations and the subjunctive

After verbs such as recommend or insist, a that-clause can use the base verb for a requested action. British English also commonly uses should plus the base verb.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says A request and a claim can start with the same verb. Listen to what the person meant. If they insisted someone do something, use the request pattern. If they insisted something was true, report the statement normally.

The pattern

recommend/insist/request + that + subject + base verb; should + base verb is another option

How does it work?

1. Use the base form in a demand or request

  1. A coach gives a strong instruction: “Everyone must arrive early.”
  2. Use “The coach insisted that everyone arrive early”.
  3. Keep arrive in the base form even though everyone is singular.

2. Make a negative request

Put not before be → “They requested that Sam not be told yet.”

3. Use ordinary tense for a claim

In “He insisted that he was right”, he said something was true. Use normal reported speech.

More examples

The coach insisted that everyone be on time. We recommend that applicants should apply early.

  • We suggest that the plan be reviewed.
  • They requested that Sam not be told yet.
  • I recommend that she should check the dates.

What to watch for

  • The base form fits a demand or recommendation. In “He insisted that he was right”, insisted means claimed something was true.
  • In the negative base-form pattern, put not before the verb: “that he not leave”.
