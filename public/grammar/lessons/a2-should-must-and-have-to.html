A2 grammar

Should, must and have to

Use should for advice and must or have to for obligations. Mustn’t means something is forbidden. Don’t have to means it is optional.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Ask “Is this advice, a rule, or a choice?” That one question helps you choose a modal. A small change to the negative can completely change what someone is allowed to do.

The pattern

should/must + base verb; have/has to + base verb

How does it work?

  1. Your friend asks for training advice: “You should bring water.”
  2. The club has a safety rule: “You have to wear a helmet.”
  3. Wearing gloves is optional: “You don’t have to wear gloves.”

More examples

You should warm up. You mustn’t touch the ball with your hands. You don’t have to bring a ball.

  • You should take a break.
  • We must leave before dark.
  • You mustn’t park here.
  • Does Alex have to pay?

What to watch for

  • Mustn’t means do not do it; don’t have to means you can choose.
  • For a past obligation, use had to: “We had to wait.”
  • Shouldn’t usually gives advice against an action.
