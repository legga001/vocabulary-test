A1 grammar

A, an and the

Use a or an for one thing when it hasn’t been identified. Choose an before a vowel sound. Use the when the listener knows which thing you mean.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Think about what your listener knows. If you mention a thing for the first time, give them a little introduction. Once they know which one you mean, you can point back to it with the.

The pattern

a/an + one countable noun; the + an identified noun

How does it work?

1. Introduce something with a or an

  1. Start a story with “A bat flew in.”
  2. Once we know which bat, say “The bat landed on my chair.”
  3. Choose “an owl” for the vowel sound at the start of owl.

2. Use an before a vowel sound

Say the word hour aloud. The h is silent, so choose an → “an hour.”

3. Use the when the listener knows which one

First say “I bought a lamp.” Now the listener knows which lamp → “The lamp is blue.”

More examples

I saw a fox. The fox had an orange tail. It waited for an hour.

What to watch for

  • Listen to the sound: a university starts with a y sound, and an hour starts with a vowel sound.
  • Use some with an amount of water. “A water” can mean a serving in a café.
