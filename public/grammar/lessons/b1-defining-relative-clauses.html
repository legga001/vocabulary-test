Defining relative clauses

Use who for people and which for things. That can often replace either in a defining clause. Leave the relative word out when it is the object.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Try taking out the extra words. If you can no longer tell which person or thing is meant, those words are doing an important job. They define the noun.

The pattern

noun + who/which/that + information needed to identify it

How does it work?

  1. Start with “I met the girl. She won the race.”
  2. Put she’s action immediately after girl and choose who.
  3. Combine them: “I met the girl who won the race.”

More examples

The coach who called me is new. The ball we bought yesterday is flat.

  • This is the bag that broke.
  • Here’s the book you lent me.
  • I know the person whose dog ran away.
  • That’s the café where we met.

What to watch for

  • You can leave out an object: “the book (that) you lent me.” Keep the word when it’s the subject: “the bag that broke”.
  • Defining clauses normally have no commas.
