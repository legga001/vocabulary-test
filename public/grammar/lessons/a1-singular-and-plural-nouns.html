A1 grammar

Singular and plural nouns

Add -s to most nouns and -es to words such as box. Some plurals change in other ways, so learn them with the noun.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says Numbers are a good clue. When there is more than one, check whether the noun follows the usual spelling or has a special plural.

The pattern

one + singular noun / two or more + plural noun

How does it work?

  1. Start with “one berry”.
  2. Berry ends in a consonant plus y, so change y to i and add es.
  3. The result is “three berries”.

More examples

One fox, two foxes. One child, two children.

  • One cup, four cups.
  • One bus, two buses.
  • One person, six people.
  • One sheep, twelve sheep.

What to watch for

  • After a vowel plus y, usually just add s: toy → toys.
  • Some forms are irregular, such as feet and mice.
