C1 grammar

Negative inversion

When a negative or limiting expression opens a sentence, put the auxiliary before the subject. Add do, does or did if the original sentence has no auxiliary. This can sound formal or dramatic.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says You can bring a negative or limiting phrase forward to make it carry more weight. Once it’s there, listen for the question-like order in the main clause. You are making a statement, but the helping verb moves forward.

The pattern

negative or limiting phrase + auxiliary + subject + main verb

How does it work?

  1. Begin with “I had never heard such a loud cheer.”
  2. Move never to the front and swap I and had.
  3. Write “Never had I heard such a loud cheer.”

More examples

Rarely do we see a fox in daylight. Only then did I realise I’d lost my key.

  • Seldom do we get a second chance.
  • Not until sunset did the wind drop.
  • Under no circumstances should you open that door.
  • Only after the meeting did we learn the result.

What to watch for

  • If only describes the subject, keep ordinary order: “Only Max knew.”
  • With “Not until we left did it rain”, the inversion is in the main clause.
  • If you add did, use the base verb after the subject.
