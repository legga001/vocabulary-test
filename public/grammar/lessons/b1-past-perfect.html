B1 grammar

Past perfect

Use had plus a past participle to show that something happened before another point in the past, especially when the order needs clarifying.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says If a story jumps back, help your listener notice. The past perfect lets you say that a second event had already happened by the time you reached the main moment.

The pattern

had + past participle for the earlier event

How does it work?

1. Show what happened first

  1. You opened the fridge at noon. Someone had eaten the cake before that.
  2. Make opening the fridge your past viewpoint.
  3. Say “When I opened the fridge, someone had already eaten the cake.”

2. Ask whether an earlier action happened

Move had before you → “Had you read the book before you watched the film?”

3. Keep two past-simple forms when the order is clear

Say “I locked the door and walked away.” The order is already clear, so locked and walked are enough.

More examples

When we reached the cinema, the film had already started.

  • They had packed before the taxi arrived.
  • I hadn’t met her before the concert.
  • Had you read the book before you saw the film?

What to watch for

  • When the order is clear, the simple past often works: “I locked up after everyone left.”
  • In a story told in order, use past simple for the next event: “I stood up and left.”
