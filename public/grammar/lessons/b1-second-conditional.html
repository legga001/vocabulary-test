B1 grammar

Second conditional

Picture an unlikely or imaginary situation with if plus a past form, then would plus the base verb for the result. If I were you is a common way to give advice.

The pattern

if + past form, would/could/might + base verb

Work through an example

  1. Suppose you don’t have a boat, but imagine that you do.
  2. Use a past form for the imagined situation: “If I had a boat”.
  3. Add the result: “If I had a boat, I’d sail to the island.”

More examples

If I had a treehouse, I’d read there every day. If I were you, I’d take a torch.

  • If we lived closer, we could meet every week.
  • If I were you, I’d ask now.
  • I’d buy it if it cost less.
  • If she knew the answer, she would tell us.

What to watch for

  • The past form can describe a situation now that you’re imagining.
  • You may hear “If I was”, but “If I were you” is the usual advice phrase.
  • Would normally goes in the result clause.

Your turn

Fix “If I would have a garden, I’d grow beans.”

Reveal the answer

If I had a garden, I’d grow beans. Put the past form in the if clause.
