C1 grammar

Fronting and information flow

Move a phrase to the beginning to connect it to the previous sentence or highlight a contrast. Keep the usual word order after many fronted objects in ordinary prose.

Let’s talk it through

Mr Fox says The first words of a sentence tell readers where to look. If you begin with the subject of the previous discussion or a detail you want to contrast, the reader can make the connection quickly.

The pattern

fronted object or related phrase + the rest of the sentence

How does it work?

1. Bring an object forward for a contrast

  1. Begin with “I can answer the first question.”
  2. Make the first question your link to what came before.
  3. Write “The first question I can answer. The second is harder.”

2. Bring a time phrase forward

Move this time to the front of “I remembered my keys this time.” → “This time, I remembered my keys.”

3. Put the place first in a description

Start with At the end of the path, then use stood a low wall. This is a descriptive order you may meet in a story.

More examples

That part I understood. The rest was a mystery.

  • That I can believe.
  • This time, I remembered my keys.
  • One thing I will say: she kept her promise.
  • At the end of the street stood a tiny bookshop.

What to watch for

  • An object moved to the front often keeps normal subject–verb order: “That I remember.”
  • Some place openings can take verb–subject order, especially in description.
  • A fronted negative phrase follows a different pattern with inversion.
