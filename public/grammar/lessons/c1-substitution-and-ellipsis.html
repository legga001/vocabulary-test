C1 grammar

Substitution and ellipsis

Avoid repeating words that are already clear. So can stand in for a clause, one can replace a countable noun, and an auxiliary can stand in for a verb phrase.

The pattern

so replaces a clause; one/ones replaces a countable noun; a helping verb can replace a verb phrase

Work through an example

  1. A friend asks “Are you coming?” and you think you are coming.
  2. Replace the repeated idea with so: “I think so.”
  3. To show a possible negative answer, say “I don’t think so.”

More examples

Will it rain? I think so. Maya can come, and Jo can too.

  • I prefer the smaller one.
  • Mia can skate, and Lee can too.
  • I didn’t bring a pen, but Jo did.
  • I asked her to stay, but she didn’t want to.

What to watch for

  • Use some or another suitable expression for an amount: “Do you have any milk? I’ll buy some.”
  • With one or ones, keep the intended noun clear. “The blue one” works when we know what object you’re choosing.

Your turn

Shorten “I can play the guitar, and my sister can play the guitar too.”

Reveal the answer

I can play the guitar, and my sister can too. Can stands in for the repeated action.
