Build a sentence

  • Subject pronouns and be

    Use I am, he/she/it is, and you/we/they are. Put not after be to make a negative. For a question, move be before the subject.

    In action
    I’m ready. They aren’t late. Is she at home?

  • A, an and the

    Use a or an for one thing when it hasn’t been identified. Choose an before a vowel sound. Use the when the listener knows which thing you mean.

    In action
    I saw a fox. The fox had an orange tail. It waited for an hour.

  • Singular and plural nouns

    Add -s to most nouns and -es to words such as box. Some plurals change in other ways, so learn them with the noun.

    In action
    One fox, two foxes. One child, two children.

  • This, that, these and those

    Use this and these for things near you. Use that and those for things farther away. These and those go with plurals.

    In action
    This hat is mine. Those boots over there are yours.

  • Possession

    Put my, your, his, her, its, our or their before a noun. Use mine or yours on its own. Add ’s to a name to show who owns something.

    In action
    That’s Mia’s scarf. Her scarf is green. The blue one is mine.

  • There is and there are

    Use there is to say one thing exists and there are for several things. For a question, start with is or are.

    In action
    There’s a ball in my bag. Are there any boots in yours?

Talk about your day

  • Present simple and frequency

    Use the present simple for habits and facts. Add -s or -es with he, she and it. Put usually or often before most verbs, but after be.

    In action
    Sam usually walks to school. He’s often early.

  • Questions and negatives with do

    Use do or does before the subject in present-simple questions with most verbs. Use don’t or doesn’t for negatives. After does or doesn’t, use the base verb.

    In action
    Does she play football? She doesn’t play on Tuesdays.

  • Present continuous

    Use am, is or are plus an -ing verb for an action happening now. Compare the action with a habit in the present simple.

    In action
    I play every Saturday. I’m playing right now!

  • Can and can’t

    Use can plus the base verb for ability or permission. For a question, move can before the subject.

    In action
    I can swim. I can’t dive. Can I sit here?

  • Commands and instructions

    Start with the base verb for a command. Use don’t plus the base verb to tell someone to avoid an action.

    In action
    Turn left at the café. Don’t cross the road yet.

  • Place and time prepositions

    Use in for an enclosed space or a month, on for a surface or a day, and at for a point or a clock time.

    In action
    My keys are on the table. See you at six on Friday!

  • Past of be

    Use was with I, he, she and it. Use were with you, we and they. Put the verb first to ask a question.

    In action
    I was at the park. Were you there too?

Quick check

The fox ___ hungry. (am / is / are)

Reveal the answer

The fox is hungry. One fox is it, so use is.
