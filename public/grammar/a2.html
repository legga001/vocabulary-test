Talk about yesterday and tomorrow

  • Past simple

    Use the past simple for a finished action. Many verbs take -ed, while others change form. After did or didn’t, switch back to the base verb.

  • Past continuous

    Use was or were plus -ing for an action in progress in the past. Use the past simple for a completed event that happened during it.

  • Going to and will

    Use be going to for an existing plan or a prediction based on evidence. Will often marks a decision made as you speak, or a promise.

  • Present continuous for arrangements

    Use am, is or are plus -ing when a future plan is already arranged. A future time expression makes the meaning clear.

  • Present perfect for experiences

    Use have or has plus a past participle to talk about an experience without naming a finished time. Use the past simple when you say when it happened.

Get things done

  • Countable and uncountable nouns

    Use a number with countable things. For uncountable things, use an expression such as a piece of or a bottle of when you need a unit.

  • Some, any, much and many

    Use some in many positive statements and offers, and any in many questions and negatives. Many goes with countable plurals. Much goes with uncountable nouns.

  • Comparatives and superlatives

    Use -er or more to compare things and -est or most to pick the one at the top of a group. Good changes to better and best.

  • Too, enough and very

    Very makes an adjective stronger. Too means more than you want or need. Put enough after an adjective or before a noun.

  • Should, must and have to

    Use should for advice and must or have to for obligations. Mustn’t means something is forbidden. Don’t have to means it is optional.

  • Verbs followed by -ing or to

    Some verbs take an -ing form after them, such as enjoy. Others, such as want, take to plus the base verb. Learn the second form with the first verb.

  • Object pronouns

    Use me, you, him, her, it, us and them after a verb or preposition.

  • Joining ideas

    Use because for a reason, so for a result and but for a contrast.
